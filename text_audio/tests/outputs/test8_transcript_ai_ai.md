## Summary
An IT worker receives mysterious, prescient emails at exactly 3:47 PM each day, which provide life-saving or crucial advice. After investigating, they discover the emails are being sent from an account on their company's server that was created using their own credentials six months prior. The narrator is left grappling with the unsettling implication that their past self is sending warnings to their future self.

## Key Takeaways
* The narrator receives eerily accurate, anonymous emails daily at 3:47 PM, a time they describe as the "longest minute" of their workday.
* The emails contain specific, helpful warnings (e.g., avoiding a traffic accident, bringing spare toner to a meeting, avoiding a faulty elevator).
* An investigation reveals the sending account was created on the company server six months ago using the narrator's own login credentials.
* The narrator concludes the only person who could know these precise details is a version of themselves from six months in the past.
* The story ends on an unresolved, meta-narrative note, as the narrator wonders if they are now compelled to become the sender, setting up warnings for their future self.

* "The longest minute of the day is 3:47 PM. Too far from 5:00 to feel hope, too near to it to worry about initiating anything new."
* "It was the only thing that happened in my day that seemed to count."
* "And some things you do not investigate because you have fear of what you will not discover. Others you do not explore because you fear to do so."

* "I'm trying very hard not to think about what I'm going to do when I get there... whether six months from now, some version of me is going to need to know something I can only find out tonight."
